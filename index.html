The problem

Why did all my Chrome tabs disappear?

If your Chrome tabs disappeared, one of these is almost always the reason. One bad restart can wipe hours of research spread across several windows.

  • Chrome crashed or froze and had to be force-quit.
  • A Windows, macOS or Chrome update restarted the computer, and Chrome reopened with a blank window.
  • Chrome showed “Chrome didn't shut down correctly” and the Restore button was missing, did nothing, or got dismissed by a stray click.
  • A new session overwrote the old one as soon as you started browsing again.
  • The wrong window was closed, taking its tab groups and pinned tabs with it.
  • A power cut or a dead battery shut the computer down mid-session.

Sauvenza keeps its own copy, whatever happens

  • Takes a snapshot of every window, tab and tab group every minute, and again 5 seconds after anything changes.
  • Stores snapshots separately from Chrome's session files, so Chrome can't overwrite them.
  • Crash Guard detects an empty restart and pins your last real session as Pre-Crash Recovery.
  • Labels the last session before every browser start Before Browser Restart and protects it from cleanup.
  • Restores everything, including tab groups and pinned tabs, with one click.

Recovery guide

How do I get all my tabs back on Chrome?

Try Chrome's own tools first. If they come up empty, Sauvenza's snapshots are the safety net, as long as it was installed before your tabs disappeared.

Restore Chrome tabs without an extension

  1. If Chrome shows “Chrome didn't shut down correctly”, click Restore straight away, before opening any new tabs.
  2. Press Ctrl+Shift+T (⌘+Shift+T on Mac) to reopen the last closed window or tabs. Press it again to go further back.
  3. Open the ⋮ menu → History → Recently closed and pick the entry with the right number of tabs.
  4. Search chrome://history (Ctrl+H) to reopen individual pages.
  5. Next time, set Settings → On startup → Continue where you left off.

Chrome keeps only its most recent session. A hard crash, a power cut, or browsing before you restore can leave nothing to bring back.

Restore Chrome tabs after a crash with Sauvenza

  1. Reopen Chrome. It doesn't matter if Chrome's own Restore prompt is missing or was dismissed: Sauvenza keeps its own copy of your session.
  2. Open Sauvenza with Ctrl+Shift+V (⌘+Shift+V on Mac) or click the toolbar icon. For 15 minutes after Chrome starts, a red ! on the icon shows that a session was preserved.
  3. Click Restore on the Session preserved banner. Every window, tab, pinned tab and tab group reopens.
  4. Need an older session? Open the dashboard, go to Auto-Snapshots, and restore any snapshot, such as Pre-Crash Recovery or Before Browser Restart.

Crash Guard

How Sauvenza makes Chrome crash-proof

Most tab managers only save what you tell them to. Sauvenza saves continuously and treats your last good session as something worth defending.

  1. Continuous snapshotsA snapshot runs on a timer (every minute by default; adjustable from 1 to 30 minutes) and 5 seconds after any tab, window or group change.
  2. Fingerprint deduplicationEach session's URL list, pinning and grouping is hashed with SHA-256. A new snapshot is saved only when that fingerprint changes.
  3. Empty-restart detectionIf Chrome comes back with just one New Tab page and the previous snapshot had more than 3 tabs, nothing is overwritten.
  4. Recovery is pinnedThe previous snapshot is relabelled Pre-Crash Recovery and pinned, so it's never pruned.
  5. You're told about itFor 15 minutes after Chrome starts, the toolbar icon shows a red ! and the popup offers Restore.

Privacy

Your tabs never leave your computer

Sauvenza is local-first. Your tabs are never sent to any server, there is no account and nothing to sync, so there is nothing to leak.

  • Tabs, workspaces and snapshots live in your browser's IndexedDB, plus any files you choose to export.
  • No analytics, no telemetry, no tracking, no third-party scripts.
  • Sauvenza only goes online for Pro: to activate or re-check a Pro key once a day (it sends the key, a random ID for your browser and the extension version), and to send the Buy Pro order form if you choose to buy.
  • Incognito windows are never saved.
  • Site icons load from the sites themselves; turn them off in Settings for letter icons only.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is Sauvenza?

Sauvenza is a crash-proof tab and session manager for Google Chrome. It automatically snapshots every open window, tab, pinned tab and tab group, restores your whole session after a crash or restart, and lets you save named workspaces you can reopen or switch between. All data stays on your computer: there is no account, no cloud sync and no tracking.

Why did all my Chrome tabs disappear?

Usually because Chrome restarted without bringing back the previous session: a crash, a forced quit, a Windows or Chrome update, a power cut, or a “Restore pages” prompt that was missing or dismissed. Chrome keeps only its most recent session, so once you start browsing again the old one can be overwritten. Sauvenza keeps its own snapshot history, saved every minute, so a lost session can still be restored when Chrome's own restore comes back empty.

How do I restore my Chrome tabs after a crash?

If Sauvenza was installed before the crash, open the Sauvenza popup (Ctrl+Shift+V, or ⌘+Shift+V on Mac) and click Restore on the “Session preserved” banner. Every window, tab, pinned tab and tab group comes back. You can also open the dashboard, go to Auto-Snapshots, and restore the snapshot labelled “Pre-Crash Recovery” or “Before Browser Restart”.

How do I restore Chrome tabs after a restart or update?

First try Chrome's own tools: click Restore on the “Chrome didn't shut down correctly” bar, press Ctrl+Shift+T (⌘+Shift+T on Mac), or open History → Recently closed. If those come up empty and Sauvenza was installed before the restart, open Sauvenza and click Restore on the “Session preserved” banner, or restore the “Before Browser Restart” snapshot from the dashboard.

Chrome says “Chrome didn't shut down correctly”. How do I get my pages back?

Click Restore on that bar straight away, before opening new tabs. If the bar disappeared or Restore did nothing, press Ctrl+Shift+T or check History → Recently closed. If Sauvenza is installed, it has already kept your previous session as “Pre-Crash Recovery” or “Before Browser Restart”, so you can restore it from the Sauvenza popup or dashboard even after Chrome's bar is gone.

I dismissed Chrome's “Restore pages” prompt. Can Sauvenza still get my tabs back?

Yes, as long as Sauvenza was running before the tabs were lost. Sauvenza keeps its own snapshot history, separate from Chrome's session files, so dismissing Chrome's prompt or starting a fresh session does not erase it. If Chrome reopens with only a New Tab page and your previous session had more than 3 tabs, Sauvenza's Crash Guard refuses to overwrite it and pins the old snapshot as “Pre-Crash Recovery”. Sauvenza cannot recover tabs from before it was installed.

How do I recover Chrome tab groups that disappeared?

Check History → Recently closed and the saved tab groups on your bookmarks bar first. Sauvenza stores each tab group's name, colour and collapsed state in every snapshot and workspace, so restoring a snapshot from before the groups vanished recreates them exactly.

Can Sauvenza restore tabs after a force quit or power cut?

Yes, as long as it was running before. Sauvenza saves a snapshot every minute and about 5 seconds after any tab change, so only changes from the last few seconds before the shutdown can be missing. After the restart, open Sauvenza and click Restore, or pick the latest snapshot in Auto-Snapshots.

Does Sauvenza upload my tabs or browsing history?

No. Tabs, workspaces and snapshots are stored only in your browser's local database (IndexedDB) and in files you choose to export. Sauvenza has no analytics, telemetry or account system. It only goes online for Pro: to activate or re-check a Pro key (once a day), and to send the Buy Pro order form if you choose to buy. Neither request contains any tab, URL or browsing data. Incognito windows are never saved.

How do I import my tabs from Toby, Session Buddy or OneTab?

Importing is a Sauvenza Pro feature. Export your data from the old extension, then open the Sauvenza dashboard, choose Import / Export, and drop in the file or paste the text. Sauvenza auto-detects Toby JSON, Session Buddy JSON or text, OneTab text, Markdown and CSV link lists, and plain URL lists. Does Sauvenza save tab groups and pinned tabs?

Yes. Snapshots and workspaces store each tab group's title, colour and collapsed state, plus which tabs are pinned. When you open or restore them, Sauvenza recreates the native Chrome tab groups exactly.

How does Sauvenza name my windows?

Sauvenza names each window after the account email it finds in that window's tabs. Webmail such as Gmail shows the signed-in address in the tab title, and Google apps can include it in the link, so a window with your work Gmail open is labelled with your work address instead of “Window 2”. A window with no visible email keeps a numbered name. The names appear in search results, saved workspaces, snapshots and Markdown, text and CSV exports.

Which browsers does Sauvenza support?

Sauvenza is built for Google Chrome 110 or later on Windows, macOS, Linux and ChromeOS. It is a standard Manifest V3 extension, so Chromium-based browsers that install extensions from the Chrome Web Store, such as Microsoft Edge, Brave, Vivaldi and Opera, can usually run it too. Firefox and Safari are not supported.

Will Sauvenza slow down Chrome?

Sauvenza runs as a Manifest V3 service worker that sleeps when idle. It wakes on a timer and about 5 seconds after tabs change, compares a SHA-256 fingerprint of your session, and writes a new snapshot only when something actually changed. It never reloads, suspends or injects scripts into your pages.

Where is my data stored, and what happens if I uninstall Sauvenza?

Everything is stored in your Chrome profile, in Sauvenza's own IndexedDB database. Chrome deletes that data when you uninstall the extension, so export a full JSON backup first (Import / Export → Full backup). Sauvenza Pro can also write an automatic backup file to Downloads/Sauvenza-Backups/sauvenza-latest-backup.json, which stays on your disk even if the Chrome profile is lost.

How do I move Sauvenza to a new computer?

On the old computer, open Import / Export and download a full backup (JSON), optionally including snapshots. Install Sauvenza on the new computer, import that file and choose Merge or Replace. Importing a backup needs Sauvenza Pro: first click Remove key from this browser in Settings & Pro on the old computer, then activate the same key on the new one. Sauvenza has no cloud sync, so your data only moves when you move the file.
